** Understanding OSINT
Open Source Intelligence, which is often referred to as OSINT, is intelligence acquired or produced from publicly available information. This information is either collected, disseminated, or exploited in a timely manner to an appropriate audience for addressing a specific intelligence requirement. Analysis involves categorizing this information, essential for investigation, background checks, identifying problem employees, or leisure. This data may one day be removed or the website hosting it may shut down unexpectedly. Applications, programs, and scripts assist in automating these tasks.

** Key Point: Data vs. Intelligence
The distinction between "Data Collection" and "Intelligence" is crucial. Finding a list of IPs is data; determining if those IPs belong to a threat actor targeting you is intelligence.

** History and Importance
OSINT stems back to the 1980s. Initially utilized by military and intelligence agencies, it involved retrieving information from publicly accessible databases, newspapers, etc. OSINT has rapidly expanded with social media, providing vast amounts of public data. For cybersecurity experts, it is vital to track this information to:

- Discover publicly accessible assets that malicious actors may leverage.
- Identify sensitive or relevant information outside the organization.
- Analyze collected data to create actionable intelligence.

Social media's extensive use allows adversaries or intelligence agencies to gather sensitive information, including facial identity from photos and movement tracking via location sharing.

** Intelligence Life Cycle
The Intelligence Life Cycle guides how intelligence is processed by civilian, law enforcement, military, and/or intelligence agencies through these phases:

1. Planning and Direction: Determines the requirements and objectives of intelligence gathering.
2. Collection: Data/information retrieval from multiple sources.
3. Processing: Analysis of collected data for comprehension.
4. Analysis and Production: Vetting information for accuracy, identifying patterns, and generating reports.
5. Dissemination and Integration: Processed information is handed over for required actions, looping back the cycle.

Why is the "Planning and Direction" phase crucial in the Intelligence Life Cycle? Skipping it leads to "analysis paralysis"—collecting irrelevant data—and potentially violating privacy laws and rules by collecting out-of-scope data.

@Bean(name = "ldapAuthenticationManager")
public AuthenticationManager ldapAuthenticationManager(List<AuthenticationProvider> providers) {
    return new ProviderManager(providers.stream().filter(provider -> {
        if (provider instanceof AuthenticationProviderToggle) {
            return ((AuthenticationProviderToggle) provider).isEnabled();
        } else {
            return true;
        }
    }).toList());
}
