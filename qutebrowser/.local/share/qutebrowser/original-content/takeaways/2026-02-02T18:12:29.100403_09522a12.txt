SCORM, the Sharable Content Object Reference Model, integrates elearning content into learning management systems (LMSs), whether they're proprietary or purchased off the shelf, and is foundational knowledge for elearning creation.

This course introduces the what, why, and how of SCORM, and the tools for creating SCORM-compliant content. Author David Rivers also covers the second generation of compliance integration, the Tin CAN API (aka the Experience API), which allows for learning events to be recorded and analyzed.

What is a learning management system (LMS)?

LMS Definition: An LMS (Learning Management System) is a web-based software application platform used to plan, implement, and assess learning activities. It serves as a place to store and manage elearning courses.
Functions of an LMS: It helps instructors create and deliver content, monitor learner participation, and assess performance. It can also include interactive features like threaded discussions, web conferencing, and discussion forums.

What is SCORM?
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


SCORM Definition: SCORM stands for Shareable Content Object Reference Model. It is a set of instructions for creating and sharing elearning content.
Purpose of SCORM: It was developed to standardize elearning content across different LMSs, ensuring interoperability and reducing duplication.
Historical Context: SCORM was developed by the Department of Defense in 1999 to address the lack of standards in elearning content across various government departments.

The history of SCORM
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


Initial Standards: The first set of SCORM standards was completed in 2001, known as SCORM 1.1, based on existing standards from various organizations.
Versions and Improvements: SCORM 1.2, introduced in 2001, included metadata specifications. SCORM 2004, first released in 2004, brought new API communication standards and learning objectives sharing. The last edition, SCORM 2004 edition four, debuted in 2009, improving operability across assets.
Next Generation: The next generation of SCORM, known as Tin Can API, Experience API, or xAPI, is currently under development and will be explored further in the course.
