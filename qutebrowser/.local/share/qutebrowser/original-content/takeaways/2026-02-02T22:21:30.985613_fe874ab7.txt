SCORM, the Sharable Content Object Reference Model, integrates elearning content into learning management systems (LMSs), whether they're proprietary or purchased off the shelf, and is foundational knowledge for elearning creation.

This course introduces the what, why, and how of SCORM, and the tools for creating SCORM-compliant content. Author David Rivers also covers the second generation of compliance integration, the Tin CAN API (aka the Experience API), which allows for learning events to be recorded and analyzed.

Defining Tin Can and Experience API, or xAPI
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


SCORM has been the dominant standard in eLearning since the late nineties but has limitations, especially with mobile and informal learning.
Tin Can API (Experience API or xAPI), introduced in 2013, addresses these limitations by allowing for more flexible and comprehensive tracking of learning experiences.
Tin Can API is preferred by IT professionals as 'Tin Can' and by eLearning professionals as 'Experience API' due to its broader application in tracking various learning activities.

How does Experience API differ from SCORM?
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


SCORM is limited to eLearning courses or learning management systems, while the Experience API (xAPI) is not.
The Experience API allows for tracking a wide range of activities, including work-related tasks, social media interactions, and game achievements.
xAPI uses activity streams to track what people are doing, similar to social networking sites like Facebook and Twitter.
Activities tracked by xAPI are often relayed to a Learning Record Store (LRS).

Understanding LRS vs. LMS
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


xAPI (Experience API) allows for tracking a wide range of learning activities, not just courses and scores.
Learning Record Store (LRS) stores xAPI activity statements, which record all learning activities.
An LRS can be integrated into an LMS or be a standalone product.
An LMS with a built-in LRS supports xAPI and manages learning delivery, while a standalone LRS only supports xAPI tracking and reporting.

The four components of the ADL vision
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


Experience Tracking: Covered by the xAPI, it tracks various learning activities.
Competency Infrastructure: Describes learning goals in terms of objectives, tasks, competencies, context, and performance standards.
Content Brokering: Tags, indexes, and delivers learning content.
Learner Profiles: Tags and describes learners to personalize learning experiences.

The Training and Learning Architecture (TLA) aims to provide a new standard for describing learning experiences, goals, content, and audiences, enhancing the overall user experience in learning management systems.
