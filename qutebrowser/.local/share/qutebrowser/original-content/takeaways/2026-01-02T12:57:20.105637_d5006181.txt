Are you interested in exploring a career as a product manager in a tech organization, but are concerned you may lack the technical background required? If you’re passionate about technology, are always thinking about new product ideas and enjoy solving problems, then the role of a technical product manager might be just right for you! There’s no straightforward path that leads to a career in technical product management and indeed the role needs a fluid skill set that requires you to wear different hats throughout the product development lifecycle—from idea generation, to coming up with creative solutions, to the implementation stage, and final rollout. In this course, Dr. Marily Nika covers what the role of a technical product manager entails and describes some of the key concepts, skills, mindsets, and frameworks that will make you a great fit for this role, with or without a technical background.

Technical concepts for PMs: APIs
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video on APIs:


Importance of APIs: Understanding APIs is crucial for a technical product manager to ensure seamless user experiences across different products.
Definition of API: An API (Application Programming Interface) allows communication between two independent sources by sending a request from product A and receiving data back from product B.
Types of APIs: Different types of APIs include those that provide answers (e.g., weather or stock value), user authentication APIs (e.g., login with social media), and APIs for online purchases (e.g., PayPal).
Practical Examples: APIs are used in various scenarios like embedding YouTube videos, ride-sharing apps using GPS and map APIs, and more.

The acquisition funnel
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


Acquisition Funnel: The acquisition funnel consists of awareness, acquisition, activation, retention, revenue, and referral stages.
Awareness: Measures how many people have heard of your product.
Acquisition: Tracks the percentage of people who search for and click on your product's page.
Activation: Represents the percentage of people who download and use your product.
Retention: Indicates the percentage of users who keep coming back, reflecting user satisfaction.
Revenue: Measures the income generated from users, through ads, freemium models, or subscriptions.
Referral: Tracks how many users bring more users to your product through referrals.

Breaking into product management
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video:


Understand the Role: Make sure you fully understand what a technical product manager does and if it's the right fit for you.
Build Experience: Gain experience in creating and launching features, even through hackathons if necessary.
Improve Storytelling: Develop your ability to explain complex concepts simply and convincingly.
Revamp Your Resume: Update your LinkedIn profile and resume to reflect relevant skills and experiences.
Network: Spread the word that you're looking for product management roles and leverage your network.
Apply Widely: Apply to many roles to increase your chances and improve your application skills.
Prepare for Interviews: Take interview preparation seriously, practice mock interviews, and understand the company's interview process.
Adopt a PM Mindset: Start thinking like a product manager by challenging the status quo, empathizing with users, being creative, advocating for your ideas, and using an analytical approach.
