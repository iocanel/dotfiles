Behind every successful technology product lies the guiding hand of the product manager. Product managers are critical leaders who coordinate all aspects of product development, from making the business case to deciding when and if a product should be retired. In this course, principal staff instructor and former Adobe Principal Product Manager Doug (Rich) Winnie provides a comprehensive overview of product management and takes you through the six stages of the product life cycle (PLC): research, plan, build, release, refine, and retire. He also explains the day-to-day activities of a product manager, introduces you to the members of the product team, walks through interactive ways to help define your product, and outlines the skills a product manager needs to have to be successful. Whether you want to become a product manager or simply become a better member of a product team, this course will help you understand how a good process brings better products to life.

This course is part of a Professional Certificate from Aha!.

Learning objectives
Identify types of products.
Recognize different types of industries.
Examine what elements make up a quality extended team.
Explore the components of managing a life cycle.
Name the elements of a strong research plan.
Break down how to pitch an idea.
Identify versions, releases, and sprints.
Recognize how to monitor progress using a burndown chart.
Define your go-to market.

Here are the key takeaways from the video on using a burndown chart to monitor progress:


Burndown Chart Usage: A burndown chart helps monitor the amount of effort put into building the product and the amount of work remaining in the sprint or release.
Data Collection: Collect data on how much time has been spent on user stories and how complete each feature is.
Chart Components: The chart includes lines for the percentage of features completed (team's velocity) and the amount of effort put in, paired with model lines indicating expected progress.
Monitoring Progress: Use the burndown chart to ensure the release is on track and to avoid burning out the build team.

Test your product with users
Ioannis Canellos:
Key takeaways for this video
Learning AI:
Here are the key takeaways from the video on testing features with users:


Specific Tests: Define clear scenarios and tasks for users to perform during testing to get valuable feedback.
Observe and Listen: Conduct tests in person to observe user behavior and encourage them to think out loud.
Avoid Direct Answers: If users ask questions during testing, prompt them to think about where they would expect to find features instead of giving direct answers.
Record Sessions: Record the screen or make a video of the test to capture user interactions and feedback for later analysis.
Validate Features: Testing helps ensure that features are validated and any issues are identified before release.
